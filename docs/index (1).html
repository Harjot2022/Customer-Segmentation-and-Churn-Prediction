🎯 Project Overview

Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis — rule-based scoring, not machine learning — segments customers into 7 groups for targeted retention. Every metric runs on the true customer identity (customer_unique_id), not the per-order key the raw data exposes by default. A separate K-means model adds exploratory, data-driven customer groups (see below).

Customer Segments

  • Champions: Best customers, highest value
  • Loyal Customers: Consistent purchasers
  • Potential Loyalist: High conversion potential
  • Need Attention: Largest segment, ~40% of customers
  • At Risk: Elevated recency, previously high value
  • New Customers: Recent, low frequency
  • Lost: Inactive for an extended period

Business Intelligence

Key Business Insights

📈

Revenue Concentration

Champions (7% of customers) generate 13.15% of revenue — a real but modest concentration, not the inflated 80/20-style split a naive analysis can produce when customer identity is computed incorrectly.

💰

Revenue at Risk

R$7.0M — 45.6% of total revenue — sits with customers in the At Risk, Need Attention, or Lost segments. No prediction model involved: this is their own historical spend, grouped by how recently and how often they've bought.

💵

Retention ROI

A hypothetical scenario: recovering 20% of the "At Risk" segment's historical spend would add roughly R$641K in revenue. It is a what-if on past spend, not a forecast, and uses no ML probability score.

Machine Learning

Exploratory ML Customer Groups

Alongside the rule-based RFM segments, a K-means model learns customer groups directly from recency, frequency, and monetary value.

⚙️

How It Works

RFM features are log-transformed (log1p) and standardized, then clustered with K-means (fixed seed, 10 initializations). The number of clusters, from 2 to 6, is chosen by silhouette score on a fixed sample of up to 2,000 customers. IDs, ratings, and RFM labels are not features.

📦

What It Produces

Four tables, exported as CSVs and Excel sheets: customer clusters, cluster profiles, model selection, and an ML-vs-RFM comparison. The fitted pipeline is saved as a joblib bundle for assigning new customers with compatible RFM features.

⚠️

What It Is Not

These are exploratory groups, not churn probabilities or lifetime-value predictions. Silhouette measures cluster separation, not predictive accuracy, and there is no held-out outcome validation. Cluster numbers are arbitrary and can change when the model is refit.

In the current run the model selected k=2 (silhouette 0.72): a small group of about 2.8K repeat buyers (roughly 3% of customers, 2.1 orders on average) and a large group of one-time buyers. A high silhouette here reflects how easy that split is, not predictive power. Cluster results can change when the model is refit.
